## Introduction

It's the big screen, and you're playing in the big league. Well, nearly. Maybe this year you'll make it big... nominations are due to be published any day now, and hey, who knows, maybe you will finally get that Oscar or Palme d'Or you so deserve. Or will you? They say girlie movies like you've been making lately are going out of fashion, that the modern public has a yearning for plotless action titles. Maybe it is time to invite that rotten art critic over for drinks, and convince her of the value of your masterpieces... or tune into the old boys' network and really get the show on the road.

In 'Cannes: Stars, Scripts & Screens', each player takes the role of a small-time movie producer, trying to produce as many movies as possible. The game can be played by 2 to 4 people and takes 45 to 90 minutes, depending on the number of players and their gaming experience.

### Setting Up

Put one or both Cannes tiles in the middle of the table. With 2 or 3 players, use one tile only. With 4, use both tiles. Put them against each other so that three sides connect.

If you are playing with two or three players, take some of the tiles out of the game and put them into the box (see the picture on the back cover). Shuffle the remaining tiles and place them face-down on a large pile on the 'draw' area. Take three markers representing three types of movies and put them on the box office index. All movies start at a popularity of 13.

Each player gets a week schedule and five rods in his own color.

The object of the game is to produce and market as many movies as possible. To do so, players need a personal network to recruit stars, get the best special effects artists, and attract great scriptwriters. These people live somewhere around Cannes, so that's where the game starts. Slowly, players add new people and places (represented by tiles) to the area. The players do their utmost best to connect to as many of these people as possible, using their own network (wooden rods), or the old boys' network. The latter are represented by cigars, and can be used by anyone that taps into them.

<!-- PDF page 3 -->

Of course, you can make new friends every turn, but you might have to give up some old ones...

Along the line of these networks, players transform ordinary human beings into stars, cranky computers into magical special effects, and the required amount of alcohol into grandiose scripts, in order to produce that pinnacle of human achievement: a movie.

### Turn Sequence

The player who can name the most movies currently showing at your local cinema may start.

Players then take turns in clockwise order. A turn consists of either

* placing two tiles OR

* placing one tile, changing your network, and producing stuff.

### Tile Placement

The player whose turn it is takes three tiles from the pile and looks at them. Other players may not see the tiles. He may then play one or two tiles on the table. If he chooses to play two tiles, this ends his turn. If he plays one tile, he may then go on to develop his network and produce stuff. The unused tiles are placed face- down on the discard pile.

Each tile must be connected to a tile already on the table by at least one side. A tile can only be placed if it fits on the table. Objects on the table that were there at the beginning of the game may not be removed: the map must be laid around them.

Most party zone tiles have 'invitations' drawn on them. If a party zone tile is placed next to another party zone tile, neither of them may show an invitation on the side that connects them. Party zone tiles bordering each other form one big party.

If a player's network is connected (see: developing a network) to a real estate agent, he or she may play one extra tile, i.e., 3 tiles and stop, or 2 tiles followed by the rest of the turn. If a player connects to 2 real estate agents, he may play 3 tiles and change his network and produce stuff. Playing more than 3 tiles per turn is not allowed.

A player is never obliged to use a real estate agent. A player connected to two real estate agents can choose freely to use one, both or none. Real estate agents have no impact on the number of tiles drawn: every player gets to draw three tiles.

When the draw pile is exhausted, the discard pile is shuffled and becomes the new draw pile. When the draw pile contains less than three tiles, the player drawing tiles empties the draw pile first, then shuffles the discard pile and completes his draw to three tiles. If that is not possible, the player has to start his turn with only one or two tiles to choose from. When all tiles have been placed on the table, the game ends after finishing the current player's turn.

### Developing A Network

A player performing this action may place two rods. Rods symbolize contacts: you can visit the places connected by them to recruit people and acquire materials for your movie.

Rods are placed so as to connect the center of two tiles. No two rods may ever connect the same two tiles, so rods of different players may never be placed parallel to one another. In addition, rods may not be placed parallel to an old boys marker.

A player must always place his rods in such a way that

<!-- PDF page 4 -->

all of his rods form one interconnected network. In practice, this means his first rod can be placed anywhere on the board, but the location of all subsequent rods is somewhat restricted.

Two rods automatically connect if they are on the same tile. In addition, rods connect if:

a. they both connect to a Cannes tile. It is not allowed to place rods connecting the Cannes tiles to each other. For all practical purposes, you may consider them to be one (large) tile.

b. they both connect to a tile containing a Phone line. If a player has a phone line in his network, he is allowed to place a rod starting from any other phone line tile. Phone lines thus allow a player to connect tiles that are far apart.

c. they both connect to the same party through an invitation. A party is a set of one or more adjoining party zone tiles. Rods cannot be placed to connect two party zone tiles. Connecting a party zone tile to adjoining normal tiles is allowed only if there is an invitation on the matching side of the party zone tile.

d. they both connect to the same 'old boys network'. An old boys network consists of tiles connected by 'old boys' markers (cigars). It can be used by all players.

If a player already has all of his rods on the map, he may move one of his rods already on the board instead of placing a new rod. The rod is simply picked up from the board and put down somewhere else. The player's rods must all remain interconnected: the connection may not be broken by taking out a rod, not even if it is restored again by replacing the rod.

A player is allowed to place or move two rods per turn in total.

### Producing Stuff

A player may use goods from his week schedule, and from any of the tiles to which he is connected, to produce stuff.

A player's network is said to be connected to

¢ all tiles on which he has rods.

all tiles in a party containing at least one tile on which the player has a rod.

¢ all tiles containing an old boy marker that is connected to the player's network.

There are two types of tiles that produce stuff: suppliers (yellow tiles) and film industry (green tiles).

Suppliers produce a number of goods each turn, as in- dicated on the tile. They produce only if a player wants to take the goods. No surplus goods ever remain on the tiles. E.g., a 2-beer brewery produces two beer. The player takes these items and places them on his week schedule. People counters are treated the same way (who says stars can determine their own destiny... .).

Note that each player can use the suppliers to which he is connected once during his turn.

Simple suppliers produce one item.

Double suppliers produce two items.

Film industry requires input, which is drawn from the player's week schedule reserves or from the suppliers a player is connected to. E.g., a special effects studio produces 1 special effect if it gets 2 computers as input.

to produce one special effect.

A film industry tile may be used multiple times during a player's turn. So the special effects studio will produce 2 special effects if it gets 4 computers, 3 if it gets 6 computers, etcetera. The resulting goods are stored in the player's week schedule. Input goods are discarded.

A player may freely determine the order in which he produces.

If a player produces movies, he or she can release these in Cannes (see: scoring), provided he is connected to a Cannes tile. He can also decide to store the movies in his week schedule for a later release.

At the end of his turn, a player may keep only 5 items on his week schedule. Any items in excess of 5 must be discarded. The player may freely decide which items to keep.

### Special Tiles

A number of tiles have special functions, which can be used by players connected to them:

A golf club allows a player to expand the old boys network.

If a player's network is connected to a golf club, he can place an old boys marker by discarding two People markers.

Old boys markers (cigars) are placed in the same way as rods, except that they may not be placed on a tile containing a phone line. They must be connected to the network of the player who puts them down, but not necessarily to other old boys markers. Like rods, an old boy may not be placed parallel to a rod or another old boy. Once produced, cigars must be placed immediate- ly. They cannot be stored on the week schedule.

As players are free to determine the order in which they produce, they may use a newly placed old boy to procure more goods, e.g., first attract people, then place an old boy to connect to a chips factory, then take a chips marker.

In later turns, a player may move his network in such a way that he disconnects from the old boys network. The

<!-- PDF page 6 -->

old boys themselves can no longer be moved once they have been played.

If all old boys markers run out, the game ends as soon as the current player has finished his turn.

A real estate agent allows a player to place one extra tile each turn, as explained above under tile placement.

The reviewer can be used to influence the score. For each beer you offer the reviewer, a player can increase the current popularity of one of the three types of movies by one step on the box office indicator. The reviewer can be used only in your own turn while you are producing stuff. The popularity of a type of movie can never rise above 15.

Cannes is important to release movies (see under scoring). Releasing movies is allowed only while you are producing stuff.

Party zone hotspots are suppliers on a party zone tile. They produce stuff for anyone connected to the party that they are in.

Phone lines are available at the reviewer and the real estate agents. As explained above, a player connected to one phone line can place rods starting from another phone line. Old boys' markers may never be placed on a tile containing a phone line. Note that a player can use the special abilities of a tile with a phone line only if he has arod on it!

### Scoring

The only way to score points is to release movies in Cannes. To do so, a player must be connected to the Cannes tile. If a movie is released, it will attract a number of visitors. The more visitors you attract, the more points you score. The revenue (score) of each type of movie is indicated by the box office index. The three types of movies have separate popularities (revenues).

For each movie published, a player takes movie tickets equal to the revenue generated. So, if the box office

<!-- PDF page 7 -->

index shows a popularity of 13 (million Euro) for girlie movies and a popularity of 10 for sci-fi movies, publishing two girlie movies and one sci-fi movie will yield 2x13 + 10=36 points worth of movie tickets.

After a player has released all movies he or she wants to, the popularity of each type of movie drops by one step for each movie of that type which was released. Popularity cannot fall below 6. It just stays at this level if more movies are released. In the example above, girlie movies would drop to 11 and sci-fi movies would go to 9.

By treating the reviewer to a beer, players can increase the popularity of a particular type of movie. The popularity increases by one step for each beer offered to the reviewer. As the order in which a player produces is at his own discretion, it is allowed to bring the price up at the reviewer and publish movies in the same turn, bringing the popularity down again.

For an example of production and scoring, see page 30.

### Game End

The game ends as soon as ¢ All tiles have been laid or ¢ All 10 old boys markers have been played.

In both cases, finish the current player's turn. Whoever has most tickets wins the game. In case of a tie, determine which potential winner would have been the next one to get a turn. That player then wins the game.

### Tactical Considerations

Remembering which tiles you have discarded (i.e., out of the three you get to choose from each turn) can be very important.

Especially with 2-3 players, it can be wise to postpone placing your first rod and first build a lucrative area.

Permanently blocking a person is very hard, especially with 2-3 players. However, forcing someone to take a detour of one extra step can already be crushing.

Party zones can be used to block, but also to connect. It is often crucial who gets the last turn. Real estate agents and golf clubs are instrumental in influencing this.

- Old boys provide a temporary advantage. Eventually, they will be used by many or all players, and just speed up the game.

## Expansion: Pirate Ship and Storing Tiles

This is a mini-expansion for Cannes: Stars, Scripts and Screens.

It introduces a new way to make money, using the pirate ship to show 'pre-release' versions of movies, as well as the ability to store tiles until later rounds.

### Pirate Ship

The pirate ship can be used to liven up any party by showing a pre-release version of movie.

When the second distinct party is created (a party is any number of connected party zone tiles), the pirate ship is placed there. The pirate ship remains in play, unless someone connects parties, so that only one distinct party remains; in this case, the ship is removed until a second party emerges.

If a player is connected to the party containing the pirate ship, he may show a pre-release version of a movie (in the production phase of his turn) by discarding a script, an actor or a special effects. For this he scores 4 (million euros). Directly after a player has shown a pre-release, the player to his left moves the pirate ship to a different party.

### Storing Tiles

As soon as a player has started his network (has at least one wooden stick on the board), he gains an additional option during the tile placement phase. Instead of adding a tile to the board, a player may decide to keep it on his week schedule. A tile uses up two spaces of the week schedule, and each player can store at most one tile at a time. A player can add a stored tile to the board in any tile placement phase. This counts as one tile placement action.
