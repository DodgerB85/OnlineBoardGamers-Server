{% translate "Mergers:" %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %} In order to propose a merger, you require a high enough merger tech, a free slot (or to own one of the merging companies), and enough money to start the bidding. If you cannot propose any mergers your turn will be skipped - you can check in the history for the reason. During your turn to propose a merger, you can click on ANY slot to see why it is not available to merge; either before or after you have selected the first slot. When placing any bid, you can select the amount you want to bid right now, plus a maximum bid, along with whether or not to pass when your maximum bid has been exceeded. If you have placed a maximum bid, you will automatically bid in the smallest increment possible, up to your maximum bid. Your maximum bid must of course be at least 2 increments higher than your immediate bid - otherwise there is no chance of you ever bidding that amount. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% translate "Operations:" %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %} You may select a slot to operate either by using the Player Table, or by clicking on a production marker / ship on the map. When operating a land company, remember you MUST ship the maximum possible goods. If you get into a situation where you can no longer ship the maximum possible goods, you will need to restart your turn and ship differently. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %} For example, if you have shipping chain 1 that can deliver to cities A and B, and shipping chain 2 that can only deliver to city B, then the most shipments can be done by using chain 1 for city A and chain 2 for city B, for a total of 2 deliveries. However, if you use chain 1 to deliver to city B, then you will be unable to ship any more goods. Thus because the maximum possible deliveries is 2, you will need to restart your turn. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %} If you only have un-operated shipping companies that cannot expand, your turn will be skipped and one of those companies will be operated at random. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% translate "Auto Shipping:" %} {% blocktranslate trimmed %} You can always Auto-Ship, which will provide you the cheapest possible way to ship the maximum possible number of goods. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% translate "You can click the \"Show Destination Cities\" button to show where the goods will be delivered." %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %} You can recalculate the auto-shipping options, as sometimes there are multiple cheapest options; for example, if you could use 1 opponent's ship to ship to city A or city B, then both routes would have the same cost. Similarly, using 1 of your own ships to ship to city A has the same cost as using 4 of your ships to ship to city B. Sometimes you may wish ship the most possible goods, but not at the lowest cost; perhaps to grow a particular city. You can always achieve this by shipping the goods manually. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %} You can also ship a good manually, using the above method, and then the auto-ship table will automatically update to show the cheapest way to ship the remaining goods. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% translate "Manual Shipping:" %} {% blocktranslate trimmed %} You click on a production marker, then a series of ships, and finally a city in order to deliver goods manually. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% translate "You may also ship using one row of the auto-ship table at a time, using the \"Copy\" button." %}

{% translate "Expert option - player selection:" %} {% blocktranslate trimmed %} This option only affects games with 3 or more players. It allows you to try to affect your relative net income compared to another player. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %} For example, if you as player A have the option to ship with player B at a cost of 10 (which would result in player B gaining a net +20 against you [-10 for you, +10 for them, is net +20]), and also the option to ship with player C at a cost of 15, then de-selecting player B will result in player C being favoured, even though the overall cost to you is slightly higher. {% endblocktranslate %}

{% translate "You can use this option to try to prevent your money going to a particular player, even if it means gaining less money overall." %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %} Note: In the above exmple, if C had cost 20 instead, then the algorithm would randomly pick between player B and C, because your relative change to player B would be -20 (either player B gains 10 and you pay 10, or else you pay player C 20). {% endblocktranslate %}

{% translate "City Growth:" %}

{% blocktranslate trimmed %} If multiple cities are able to grow, and there are not enough city markers to grow all of them, then the first player in turn order will be asked to choose which cities should upgrade. {% endblocktranslate %}
