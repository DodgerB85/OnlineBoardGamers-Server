# Great Western Trail (ROW) — Rules Specification

Authoritative rules reference for the engine. Source: `ROW_Rules_GB_LR.pdf` (base game, 2–4 players). It covers components, setup, the full turn sequence, every action type, the neutral/private building catalog, Kansas City, objective cards, end-game scoring, and edge cases.

Scope: base game only. Rails to the North and solo/automa content are not covered by this file.

## 2. Setup

### 2.1 Board and neutral tiles

1. Place the game board in the middle of the table.
2. Shuffle the 5 station master tiles and place one face up at random on each of the 5 station master spaces.
3. Take the 7 neutral building tiles and place one face up on each of the 7 neutral spaces:
   - First game: place each tile on the space marked with its matching letter (A–G).
   - Later games: shuffle the tiles and distribute them at random.

### 2.2 Kansas City supply

4. Place the job market token on the top dashed round space in the job market.
5. Take the 54 worker tiles, 22 teepee tiles and 18 hazard tiles. Turn them face down and sort them into three piles by the number on their back (1, 2, 3). Shuffle each pile separately and place the three face-down piles by the upper-left of the board, next to Kansas City. These piles are the **Kansas City supply**.

### 2.3 Board tiles, job market and foresight

6. Reveal tiles from the back-1 pile and place them on the board according to type:
   - **Teepee tile (green or blue):** place it in the Indian trade section on the empty space with the lowest printed money value (the track starts at −3).
   - **Hazard tile:** place it in its matching hazard section (flood, drought or rockfall) on the empty space with the lowest number (1–4).
   - If there is no empty space for a tile, flip it face down and return it to the Kansas City supply.
   - Keep revealing back-1 tiles until 7 tiles have been placed on the board.
7. Reveal back-2 tiles (these are worker tiles) and seed the job market:
   - Start in the top row, on the space directly beneath the player count sign for the actual player count.
   - Place the first worker there, then continue rightwards.
   - After filling the rightmost space of the top row, continue on the second row, again starting on the space in line with the player count sign, and continue rightwards.
   - The last tile is placed on the space immediately to the left of the job market token.
   - The number of seeded workers is therefore between 3 and 7, depending on player count.
8. Fill the 6 foresight spaces in Kansas City with tiles from the Kansas City supply:
   - 2 tiles with back number 1 on the two foresight spaces marked 1.
   - 2 tiles with back number 2 on the two foresight spaces marked 2.
   - 2 tiles with back number 3 on the two foresight spaces marked 3.
   - All 6 tiles are face up.

### 2.4 Markets and displays

9. Shuffle the 36 market cattle cards and place them as a face-down **market cattle stack** beneath the lower-left of the board.
   - Draw 7 cards in a 2-player game, 10 in a 3-player game, or 13 in a 4-player game.
   - Sort the drawn cards by color and place them face up in an overlapping row in the **cattle market**. The order from left to right is yellow, red, blue, brown, purple. Not every color has to be present.
   - The left side of each card must remain visible.
10. Shuffle the 24 objective cards and place them as a face-down **objective stack** to the right of the board. Reveal 4 cards face up in a column below the stack. This is the **general objective display**.
11. Place the coins within reach of all players as the **bank**.

### 2.5 Players

12. Each player chooses a color and takes the matching player board. If playing with 3 or 4 players, each player also takes the player count tile, turns it to the side for the actual player count, and uses it to cover Phase A of the player board. In a 2-player game no player count tile is used.
13. Each player takes the 10 private building tiles of their color:
    - In the first few games, turn all tiles to their a-side and display them in ascending order above the player board.
    - Alternatively, one player randomly determines the side of each tile and all other players copy that display.
    - For the rest of the game, the side of each building tile is identical for all players. A building tile may never be deliberately flipped to its other side.
14. Each player receives the following pieces:
    - **14 player discs:** place them on the colored disc spaces of the player board. Only the 2 gray spaces in the upper-left remain empty.
    - **1 engine:** place it on the starting space (the red building) of the railroad track.
    - **1 certificate marker:** place it on the top space of the certificate track, next to the 0.
    - **1 cattleman:** place it next to the player board for now.
15. Shuffle the 4 starting objective cards and deal one random card to each player. Place it face up below the player board. This is the first card of that player's **personal objective area**. Return unused starting objective cards to the box.
16. Each player takes the 14 player cattle cards marked with their color's star. Shuffle them and place them as the face-down **personal draw stack** to the left of the player board. Draw 4 cards from it to form the starting hand.
17. Determine the starting player. The starting player receives $6 from the bank. The next player clockwise receives $7, the third player (if any) receives $8, and the fourth player (if any) receives $9.

### 2.6 First turn

On the very first turn of the game, the starting player places their cattleman on any neutral building tile and continues directly with Phase B. A player may start on the same neutral building tile as another player. Phase A is not performed on that first turn; Phase C is still performed after Phase B.

## 4. State Model

### 4.1 Player state

Each player has:

- **Money:** dollars in front of the player.
- **Herd deck:**
  - `drawStack`: face-down personal draw stack.
  - `hand`: cards in hand, capped by the hand limit.
  - `discardPile`: face-up personal discard pile.
- **Hand limit:** starts at 4, maximum 6.
- **Step limit:** starts at 3 or 4 depending on player count, maximum 6.
- **Certificate track:**
  - Temporary certificate limit: starts at 3, maximum 6.
  - Certificate marker position: starts at 0.
  - Permanent certificates: +1 breeding value each for the rest of the game, from station master tiles.
- **Worker section:** 3 rows (cowboys, craftsmen, engineers) with 6 spaces each. The first space of each row is occupied by a starting worker.
- **Private buildings:** 10 tiles, each with a fixed a-side or b-side, either unplaced, placed on the board, or removed from the game.
- **Discs:** 14 discs. A disc is either on a player-board disc space or placed on a city crest or train station.
- **Cattleman:** current trail location.
- **Engine:** current railroad track space.
- **Personal objective area:** face-up objective cards that have been played.
- **Collected tiles:** face-up hazard tiles, teepee tiles, and station master tiles in front of the player.
- **Player-board state:** which disc spaces are cleared.

Note: the printed starting worker on each row is a worker for all counting and scoring purposes. It is not a separate resource.

### 4.2 Board state

- **Trail:** directed graph of locations. A location is any tile placed on a trail space or Kansas City. Empty trail spaces are passing segments, not locations.
- **Location data:** tile type, building owner and tile number/side, green/black hand icons, attached risk action, adjacent locations, building space or not.
- **Railroad track:** starting space plus spaces 1–39, turnouts belonging to train stations, engines, station discs, station master tiles.
- **Job market:** rows, row width, worker tiles, job market token position.
- **Cattle market:** face-up row of market cattle cards and the face-down market cattle stack.
- **Objective stack and general objective display.**
- **Kansas City supply:** three face-down piles sorted by back number 1/2/3.
- **Foresight:** 3 spots, each with 2 face-up tiles. Spot 1 uses back number 1, spot 2 uses back number 2, spot 3 uses back number 3.
- **Indian trade section:** face-up teepee tiles with printed money values.
- **Hazard sections:** flood, drought and rockfall, each with spaces numbered 1–4.
- **Neutral buildings A–G** and **station master spaces**.

### 4.3 Global state

- Current player and turn order.
- Starting player.
- Whether the game-end trigger has occurred.
- Job market token holder.
- Whether a player has already upgraded each train station.

## 5. Turn Sequence

Players take turns clockwise, beginning with the starting player (the player who received $6). A turn consists of exactly these phases, in this order:

1. **Phase A — Move** the cattleman to another location along the trail.
2. **Phase B — Use** the action(s) of the reached location.
3. **Phase C — Draw** up to the hand limit.

Then the next player's turn begins. No phase may be skipped or interleaved, except for the special first turn described in section 2.6.

---

## 6. Phase A — Movement

### 6.1 Locations and steps

- Each tile placed on a trail space is a location: neutral building, private building, hazard tile or teepee tile. Kansas City is also a location.
- Empty trail spaces are not locations and do not count as steps.
- Every location passed over or stopped on counts as exactly 1 step, regardless of how many empty spaces lie between locations.
- Movement is always forward along the arrows printed on the trail. Backward movement is illegal in Phase A.
- If the trail forks, choose one of the available paths.
- The cattleman must move at least 1 step and may not move more than the current step limit.
- The cattleman ends movement either voluntarily or because the step limit is exhausted. The location where it ends is the location used in Phase B.
- Kansas City may not be passed over. If the cattleman reaches Kansas City, movement ends there and any unused steps are forfeited.
- Other players' cattlemen do not block movement. Several cattlemen may occupy the same location.

### 6.2 Hand fees

Some locations show a green hand, a black hand, or both. When the cattleman moves onto or passes over such a location, the fee is paid immediately, once per hand shown.

| Player count | Green hand | Black hand |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2 players | $2 | $2 |
| 3 players | $2 | $1 |
| 4 players | $1 | $2 |

Fee recipients:

- **Hazard tile or teepee tile:** pay the bank.
- **Another player's private building tile:** pay that player.
- **Your own private building tile:** no fee, even if it shows a hand.
- **A tile showing both a green and a black hand:** pay both fees.

If the player cannot pay some or all hand fees along the path, the player pays as much as possible and continues moving. The player's money becomes $0 if necessary. Hand fees are never owed retroactively, even if the player receives money later while still at that location.

This underpayment rule applies only to hand fees. Every other cost and requirement in the game must be paid or met in full.

---

## 7. Phase B — Use the Reached Location

The options depend on the type of location where the cattleman ended movement.

### 7.1 Neutral building or own private building

The player has two mutually exclusive options:

1. **Use the local action(s) of the tile.**
2. **Use exactly one single auxiliary action** from the player board.

The auxiliary action is available only if the player performs none of the tile's local actions. If the player performs even one local action, the auxiliary action is forfeited.

#### Local actions

- Most building tiles show two local actions on their lower half, separated by full-length dividers. A few show one action or more than two.
- Each local action of the tile may be performed once.
- Local actions may be performed in any order.
- The player does not have to perform every local action. Individual actions may be forfeited.
- If an action shows a cost or requirement, it must be paid or met completely to gain that action's reward.
- If a single local action shows two alternatives separated by a white slash, only one of those alternatives may be performed.
- If the building space has a **risk action** printed on it, that risk action becomes part of the building's local actions. The player using the building may perform it as one of that building's local actions each time they reach that location.

#### Auxiliary action on a building

- Only one single auxiliary action may be used, never a double action, even if both disc spaces of that auxiliary action are cleared.
- The auxiliary action must be unlocked (see section 10.10).

### 7.2 Another player's private building, hazard tile or teepee tile

The player may not use any local tile action. The only option is **one single auxiliary action** from the player board. It may never be doubled.

### 7.3 Kansas City

When the cattleman reaches Kansas City, the player must carry out all 5 Kansas City subphases in numerical order.

#### Subphase 1 — Foresight 1

Choose one of the 2 tiles in the foresight spaces below spot 1 and immediately deploy it to its matching section:

- **Worker tile:** place it on the next free space of the job market (see section 7.4).
- **Teepee tile:** place it in the Indian trade section on the empty space with the lowest money value. If no space is left, remove the tile from the game (return it to the box).
- **Hazard tile:** place it in its matching hazard section on the empty space with the lowest number (1–4). If no space is left, remove the tile from the game (return it to the box).

#### Subphase 2 — Foresight 2

Same as subphase 1, using the 2 tiles below spot 2.

#### Subphase 3 — Foresight 3

Same as subphase 1, using the 2 tiles below spot 3.

#### Subphase 4 — Income

1. Reveal the player's entire hand.
2. Sum the breeding values of each **different cattle type** in hand. Each type counts only once, no matter how many cards of that type are held. Duplicate types contribute nothing extra.
3. Ignore victory-point icons on cattle cards and ignore objective cards for this calculation.
4. The player may add temporary certificates: move the certificate marker backwards by any number of spaces up to the number of temporary certificates available. Each certificate used adds +1 to the total.
5. Each permanent certificate from a station master tile adds +1 automatically.
6. The player takes that many dollars from the bank.
7. Discard the entire hand to the personal discard pile.

#### Subphase 5 — Delivery

1. Choose a city along the railroad whose **city value** is equal to or lower than the player's total breeding value.
2. The player may not choose a city that already contains one of their own discs, except **Kansas City** and **San Francisco**. Those two cities may be chosen repeatedly.
3. Take one player disc from any disc space on the player board:
   - A disc from a **white-corner** space may be placed on any city space.
   - A disc from a **dark-corner** space may only be placed on a city space that also has dark corners.
   - If the player must place a disc on a white-corner space but only has dark-corner discs left, a dark-corner disc may be placed on the white-corner space.
   - If the player has no disc left or cannot pay the required cost, remove one of their discs from a train station of their choice and place that disc instead.
4. Place the disc on the chosen city space, on top of any discs already there.
5. Resolve any delivery actions triggered by the placement:
   - A **green arrow** between two city crests is activated only when both adjacent crests have one of the player's discs. The action then must be performed immediately: choose one objective card from the general objective display, place it face up on the personal discard pile, and refill the display if necessary.
   - If placing the disc completes both adjacent green arrows, perform the action twice. Refill the general objective display before the second action.
   - Victory points printed in a green arrow are unlocked only when both adjacent crests have one of the player's discs.
   - Placing a disc on the **Kansas City** crest immediately gives $6 from the bank. At final scoring, that disc is worth −6 VP.
   - Other printed victory points and negative victory points on city crests are scored at the end of the game.
6. Pay transport costs:
   - If the nose of the player's engine is beneath or farther up the railroad track than the chosen city crest, the cost is $0.
   - If the engine is farther down the track than the city crest, pay $1 to the bank for each cross located between the nose of the engine and the city value of the crest.
   - Income is received before delivery, so transport costs can always be paid.
7. After delivery, move the cattleman back to the start of the trail, on the horseman space in the lower-right corner.
8. Refill each of the 3 empty foresight spaces in Kansas City with a face-up tile from the Kansas City supply whose back number matches the number of that space.

### 7.4 Deploying a worker tile from foresight

- The worker is always placed in the row where the job market token is currently located.
- Spaces in that row are filled from left to right.
- Row widths depend on player count:
  - 2 players: 2 spaces per row, starting beneath the 2-player count sign. Both left columns remain empty.
  - 3 players: 3 spaces per row, starting beneath the 3-player count sign. The left column remains empty.
  - 4 players: 4 spaces per row.
- The last space of each row is the space with the job market token. When a worker is placed on that space, move the job market token along the arrow to the dashed round space of the next row.
- If the job market token moves along a **yellow arrow**, immediately refill the cattle market to its initial size (7/10/13 depending on player count). Draw from the market cattle stack and sort the new cards by color (yellow, red, blue, brown, purple). If the cattle market already has at least its initial number of cards, add nothing. If the market cattle stack is empty, add nothing.
- If the job market token moves from the bottom row along the **red arrow**, the game-end trigger occurs (see section 14).

### 7.5 Phase B again

Some private building actions and objective card immediate actions move the cattleman forward and instruct the player to perform Phase B again at the new location. The hand is **not** replenished before this second Phase B; replenishment happens only in Phase C at the end of the turn. If the new location is Kansas City, the full Kansas City subphase sequence is performed.

---

## 8. Phase C — Draw Up to the Hand Limit

1. Compare the number of cards in hand with the current hand limit.
2. If the hand contains fewer cards than the hand limit, draw cards from the personal draw stack until the hand reaches the limit.
3. If the draw stack is empty when a card must be drawn, shuffle the personal discard pile thoroughly and place it face down as the new draw stack. Then continue drawing. The discard pile is never shuffled into a new draw stack at any other time.
4. If the deck does not contain enough cards to reach the hand limit even after shuffling, the player simply continues with fewer cards.
5. If the player just completed the Kansas City subphases, the entire hand must be replenished.
6. Then the next player's turn begins.

## 9. General Action Rules

These rules apply to every action, regardless of whether it comes from a building tile, an auxiliary action, a station master tile, an objective card or a delivery.

### 9.1 Requirements and rewards

- Requirements are shown in red (red arrows or red numbers). Rewards are shown in green or white (green arrows or white numbers).
- A requirement must be met completely to gain the associated reward.
- A reward may be forfeited completely or in part, except a dollar reward: if the player receives dollars as a reward, the entire amount must be taken.
- If an action has a cost, the cost must be paid in full.

### 9.2 Discarding cards

- "Discard a card" always means: take the card from the hand and place it face up on the personal discard pile.
- Cards gained during the game are placed face up on the personal discard pile, unless the specific action says to add the card directly to the hand.
- The personal discard pile is open information; the personal draw stack is not.
- A player may look through their personal discard pile at any time, but may not look at the cards in their personal draw stack.

### 9.3 Certificate marker

- "Move the certificate marker forward" means moving it forward on the certificate track by the number of spaces shown on the action icon.
- The marker may never move beyond the current temporary certificate limit.
- Each station master tile with a permanent certificate adds +1 to the breeding value calculation automatically for the rest of the game.

### 9.4 Engine movement

- Engine movement is along the spaces of the railroad track.
- The standard engine-move action allows moving forward up to the number of engineers in the engineer row.
- Other icons specify a fixed number of spaces, a number based on another value, or a backward movement; use the number shown on that icon.
- Except for the starting space, no track space may hold more than one engine. A space occupied by another engine is ignored entirely, as if it did not exist.
- Turnout spaces count as one additional space between the two spaces they emerge from. Coming from a space with a switch, an engine may move to the next numbered space or to the turnout space, provided the turnout is not blocked.
- An engine may always move fewer spaces forward than allowed. Once it stops on a space, any unused movement from that action is forfeited.
- When an engine is required to move backwards, the same occupied-space rule applies: occupied spaces are ignored, which can cause the engine to move farther backwards. A backward-moving engine may stop on a turnout space and upgrade that station.
- Backward movement is a requirement: the player may not deliberately move fewer spaces backwards than required.
- If the engine is on the starting space of the railroad track, an action that requires backward movement cannot be used.
- The engine is never turned around. Its nose always faces forward, even while moving backwards.

### 9.5 Acquired cards

- Market cattle cards acquired through the Buy Cattle action are placed face up on the personal discard pile.
- Objective cards acquired through the Gain Objective Card action are placed face up on the personal discard pile.
- Exception: private building **6b** adds the objective card directly to the hand.

### 9.6 Action frequency

- Each local action of a building tile may be used once per visit.
- A single action may not be repeated just because its requirement could be met multiple times.
- Auxiliary actions are used once per action that grants them.

## 10. Action Catalog

### 10.1 Hire one worker

- Choose exactly one worker tile from the job market.
- A worker may not be hired from the row where the job market token is currently located. Those workers are unavailable until the token moves on.
- The hiring cost is the cost printed on the right side of the worker's row, modified by the action icon:
  - `0` — cost unchanged.
  - `+2` — costs $2 more.
  - `−1` — costs $1 less.
- Pay the hiring cost to the bank.
- Place the worker on the leftmost free space of its matching row type (cowboy, craftsman or engineer).
- Each player starts with one worker of each type, so the first space of each row is already occupied.
- If the space where the worker is placed shows an immediate action, the player must either perform that action immediately or forfeit it.
- If the row is already full, the worker cannot be hired.
- At final scoring, each worker on the fifth or sixth space of any row is worth 4 VP.
- If a worker is removed from a worker-section space later (for example, to appoint it as a station master), the immediate action on that space is uncovered again and can be performed the next time another worker is placed there.

### 10.2 Place or upgrade a private building

When an action allows placing a private building tile, choose one of the player's unplaced private building tiles and either:

- place it on any empty building space on the trail, or
- replace one of the player's own private building tiles already on the trail with a higher-valued tile.

Rules:

- The side of each building tile is fixed for the whole game. A tile may never be flipped to its other side.
- For a new placement, the player must have at least as many craftsmen in the craftsman row as the number printed in the tile's upper-left corner.
- Pay $2 per craftsman required by the tile to the bank, then place the tile.
- For a replacement, the new tile must have a higher value than the tile being replaced. The player only needs the craftsman **difference** between the two tiles, and only pays $2 per point of that difference.
- The replaced tile is removed from the game and returned to the box.
- A player may only replace their **own** private building tile, not an opponent's.
- The placement action is available on neutral building tile B and through the immediate actions of the craftsman row. The craftsman-row version pays only $1 per craftsman instead of $2.
- A building tile placed on a trail space with a printed risk action gains that risk action as an additional local action.

### 10.3 Move the engine

The standard engine action moves the engine forward up to the number of engineers in the engineer row. Fixed-distance and other engine icons are described in the tile catalog.

### 10.4 Upgrade a train station and acquire a station master

- If the engine stops on a turnout space belonging to a train station, the player must immediately decide whether to upgrade that station.
- A station may only be upgraded if the player has not upgraded it before. This is recognised by the absence of that player's disc on the station. Opponents' discs do not prevent an upgrade.
- To upgrade, pay the upgrade cost printed next to the station, then place one player disc on the station space:
  - A white-corner disc may be placed on any station space.
  - A dark-corner disc may only be placed on a station space with dark corners.
  - The same fallback rules as city delivery apply if only dark-corner discs remain or no disc is available.
- After placing the disc, if a station master tile is placed next to that station, the player may acquire it immediately:
  - Choose one of the player's hired workers that is on the **rightmost occupied space** of its row.
  - Remove that worker from the player board and place it on the station master space.
  - Take the station master tile and place it face up in front of the player.
- The station master tile can only be acquired at the moment of the station upgrade. If the chance is forfeited, it is lost forever.
- Once a worker tile is placed on a station master space, it remains there for the rest of the game.
- Each station master tile has two halves:
  - **Upper half:** an immediate action or a permanent certificate. An immediate action is performed immediately or forfeited. A permanent certificate adds +1 to breeding value for the rest of the game.
  - **Lower half:** a unique end-game scoring task (see section 15.8).

The known station master upper halves are:

- A permanent certificate (+1 breeding value for the rest of the game).
- Remove a hazard free of charge, OR trade with the Indians.
- Gain $2.

### 10.5 The last railroad space (space 39)

- When an engine reaches space 39, its forward movement ends there. The player may upgrade the train station as usual.
- The player must then move the engine backwards at least one space, to any free space along the track, including a turnout space.
- After moving backwards, the player immediately gains $3 from the bank.
- If the engine moved to a turnout space, the player may upgrade that station as usual, including using the $3 just received.

### 10.6 Trade with the Indians

- Remove one teepee tile of the player's choice from the Indian trade section and place it face up in front of the player.
- Depending on the money value printed above the teepee tile, the player either gains that many dollars from the bank or pays that many dollars to the bank.
- If the Indian trade section is empty, the action cannot be performed.
- Collected teepee tiles remain in front of the player and are used for objective tasks and final scoring.

### 10.7 Remove a hazard

- Remove one hazard tile of the player's choice from any hazard section and place it face up in front of the player.
- If the action icon shows a cost, the player must pay that many dollars to the bank; otherwise the hazard cannot be taken.
- If the icon shows no cost, the hazard is taken free of charge.
- Each hazard tile is worth 2, 3 or 4 VP at final scoring.
- If all three hazard sections are empty, the action cannot be performed.

### 10.8 Buy cattle from the cattle market

When this action is performed, the player may acquire cattle cards from the cattle market and place them face up on the personal discard pile. The number and types of cards are limited by the number of cowboys in the cowboy row and by the money spent. Each cowboy may be used only once during the action.

The available options are:

| Cowboys used | Cards acquired | Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 1 cattle card with breeding value 3 | $6 |
| 2 | 1 cattle card with breeding value 3 | $3 |
| 3 | 2 cattle cards with breeding value 3 | $5 total |
| 1 | 1 West Highland cattle card | $12 |
| 3 | 1 West Highland cattle card | $6 |
| 5 | 2 West Highland cattle cards | $8 total |
| 2 | 1 Texas Longhorn cattle card | $12 |
| 4 | 1 Texas Longhorn cattle card | $6 |

- Cowboys may be split among several options, as long as no cowboy is used more than once.
- Only cattle cards currently available in the cattle market may be bought.
- The player may always buy fewer cattle cards than allowed.
- Each unused cowboy may instead be used to draw 2 cards from the market cattle stack and add them face up to the cattle market.
- All acquired cattle cards go face up on the personal discard pile.
- Buying cattle does not refill the cattle market by itself. The market is refilled only by a yellow job-market arrow or another effect that explicitly does so.

### 10.9 Gain one objective card from the general objective display

- Choose one objective card from the general objective display, OR draw the top card of the objective stack.
- Place the card face up on the personal discard pile.
- If a card was taken from the general objective display, immediately refill its vacant place with a new face-up card from the objective stack.
- If the objective stack is empty, the display is not refilled. If the display is empty as well, no objective card can be gained.

### 10.10 Auxiliary actions

Each player board has 5 auxiliary actions. Each auxiliary action has 2 disc spaces next to it:

- While both disc spaces are occupied, the action is locked.
- Clearing at least one disc space unlocks the action for single use.
- Clearing both disc spaces unlocks the action for single or double use.
- At the start of the game only 2 auxiliary actions are available. The others unlock through disc clearing.
- When an auxiliary action is used on a location instead of that location's local actions, it can never be doubled, even if both disc spaces are cleared.

| Auxiliary action | Single | Double |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Gain money | Gain $1 | Gain $2 |
| Draw and discard | Draw 1 card from the personal draw stack, then discard 1 card | Draw 2 cards, then discard 2 cards |
| Pay, move engine back, move certificate forward | Pay $1, move engine 1 space backwards, move certificate marker 1 space forward | Pay $2, move engine 2 spaces backwards, move certificate marker up to 2 spaces forward |
| Pay and move engine forward | Pay $1, move engine 1 space forward | Pay $2, move engine 2 spaces forward |
| Move engine back and remove cards | Move engine 1 space backwards, remove 1 hand card from the game (return it to the box) | Move engine 2 spaces backwards, remove 2 hand cards from the game (return them to the box) |

All engine movement rules from section 9.4 apply, including the starting-space restriction on backward movement.

### 10.11 Move the cattleman forward (building action)

- The cattleman moves forward along the trail to another location.
- The player must move at least 1 step and may not move more than the number of steps shown on the icon.
- On the newly reached location, the player performs Phase B again.
- The hand is not replenished before this second Phase B. Replenishment happens only in Phase C.
- If the new location is Kansas City, the full Kansas City subphase sequence is performed.

### 10.12 Extraordinary delivery (private building action)

1. Move the engine one or more spaces backwards.
2. Choose a city crest whose city value is equal to or lower than the number of spaces the engine just moved backwards. Occupied track spaces and turnout spaces do not count towards that number.
3. The usual city restrictions apply: the player may not choose a city that already has one of their discs, except Kansas City or San Francisco.
4. Place one player disc from the player board on the chosen city crest, following the usual white-corner/dark-corner rules.
5. Resolve any triggered delivery actions as usual.
6. No transport costs are paid.
7. If the engine landed on a turnout space, the player may upgrade that train station following the usual rules.
8. The local action then ends.

### 10.13 Copy the local actions of an adjacent building (private building 8b)

- Choose one adjacent building tile. It may be neutral, own, or another player's.
- Adjacent means there is no other location and no empty space between the two buildings.
- The player may use the copied tile's local actions as if they were printed on the current tile. The copied actions follow all normal rules and limits.

### 10.14 Upgrade a train station behind the engine (private building 9b)

- The player may upgrade one train station anywhere on the railroad track behind the engine, following the usual station upgrade rules.
- The usual restrictions apply: the station must not already have the player's disc, the upgrade cost must be paid, and the disc placement must follow the white-corner/dark-corner rules.

## 12. Objective Cards

### 12.1 Gaining objective cards

- Objective cards are gained through actions such as "Gain one objective card from the general objective display" and private building 6b.
- A gained card is placed face up on the personal discard pile, unless the action says to add it directly to the hand. It therefore enters the hand later through normal drawing.
- If a card is taken from the general objective display, refill the display immediately from the objective stack.

### 12.2 Playing objective cards

During the player's own turn, an objective card may be played from hand only:

- before performing Phase A, or
- before or after performing any one action in Phase B.

Objective cards may not be played in the middle of an action, and may not be played after Phase C has begun. Any number of objective cards may be played at each allowed window, one at a time.

When an objective card is played:

1. Place it face up in the personal objective area below the player board.
2. Perform the immediate action printed in the card's upper-left corner, or forfeit it.

A player is not compelled to play objective cards and may keep them in the deck. For each objective card still in the deck at the end of the game, the player decides whether to remove it from the game or add it to the personal objective area (without using its immediate action). Objective cards already in the personal objective area must be scored.

### 12.3 Objective card immediate actions

| Immediate action | Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| Move cattleman | Move the cattleman up to 3 steps forward along the trail, ignoring all green and black hands. The player may not use the location where the cattleman stops. If this is done before Phase A, the normal Phase A movement then starts from that location. If this is done after using a location in Phase B, Phase C is carried out directly once the cattleman reaches the new location. The cattleman may not move to Kansas City via this immediate action. |
| Draw and discard | Draw up to 3 cards from the personal draw stack. Then discard as many cards as were drawn. |
| Move engine | Move the engine up to 2 or up to 3 spaces forward, as shown on the card. |
| Gain money | Gain $2. |
| Auxiliary action | Perform one single or one double auxiliary action. |

### 12.4 Objective card tasks

Each objective card shows a combination of tasks. All tasks on the card must be fulfilled at the end of the game to gain the card's positive victory points. If the tasks are not fulfilled completely, the card's negative victory points are scored instead.

The possible tasks are:

- Have 1 of your private building tiles on the game board.
- Have 1 green teepee tile in front of you.
- Have 1 blue teepee tile in front of you.
- Have 1 hazard tile in front of you (of any kind).
- Have 1 of your discs on a train station.
- Have 1 cattle card with a breeding value of 3 in your deck.
- Have 1 West Highland cattle card in your deck.
- Have 1 Texas Longhorn cattle card in your deck.
- Have 1 of your discs on the city space of San Francisco.

Each single fulfilled task can count towards only one objective card. If several objective cards show the same task, that task must be fulfilled separately for each card.

The starting objective card has no negative victory-point value.

## 13. Player Board and Disc Spaces

The player board has 14 colored disc spaces. At setup, 14 discs cover all colored spaces. The 2 gray spaces in the upper-left remain empty.

Clearing a disc space means taking the disc off it, usually by delivering to a city or upgrading a train station. Clearing a space unlocks its ability. Some dark-corner spaces have additional requirements or give additional rewards.

### 13.1 Auxiliary action section

- The player board contains 5 auxiliary actions, each with 2 disc spaces.
- While both disc spaces are occupied, the action is locked.
- Clearing at least one disc space unlocks the action.
- Clearing both disc spaces allows the action to be used as a single or double action.
- At the start of the game only 2 auxiliary actions are available; the rest unlock through disc clearing.

### 13.2 Step-limit spaces

- The player board has 2 step-limit disc spaces.
- Clearing a space increases the step limit by the value shown on the player count tile (or on the printed Phase A for a 2-player game).
- For 2–3 players the values are +2 and +1, taking the step limit from 3 to 5 and then 6.
- For 4 players the values are +1 and +1, taking the step limit from 4 to 5 and then 6.
- When a disc is taken from a step-limit space, the player immediately gains $3 from the bank.
- Each cleared step-limit space is worth 3 VP at final scoring.

### 13.3 Hand-limit spaces

- The player board has 2 hand-limit disc spaces.
- Clearing a space increases the hand limit by 1, from 4 to 5 and then to 6.
- When a disc is taken from a hand-limit space, the player must immediately pay $5 to the bank.
- If the player cannot pay $5, another disc space must be chosen instead.

### 13.4 Certificate-limit spaces

- The player board has a 4-certificate space and a 6-certificate space.
- Clearing the 4-certificate space raises the temporary certificate limit to 4.
- Clearing the 6-certificate space raises the temporary certificate limit to 6.
- If the 6-certificate space is cleared before the 4-certificate space, the temporary certificate limit remains 3 until the 4-certificate space is also cleared.

### 13.5 Dark-corner spaces

Some disc spaces have dark corners. They follow the normal white-corner/dark-corner placement rules when their disc is placed on a city or station. Some dark-corner spaces also have additional requirements or rewards, which are printed on the player board.

### 13.6 Disc placement fallbacks

When a disc must be placed on a city space during delivery or on a station space during an upgrade:

- If the player must place a disc on a white-corner space but only has dark-corner discs left, a dark-corner disc may be placed on the white-corner space.
- If the player has no disc left or cannot pay a required cost, remove one of the player's discs from a train station of their choice and place that disc instead.

## 14. End of the Game

### 14.1 Trigger

The game end is triggered when a player places a worker tile on the last space of the job market while carrying out Kansas City subphase 2 or 3.

- This moves the job market token along the red arrow out of the job market.
- The active player immediately takes the job market token and places it in front of them.
- The active player completes the current turn:
  - If the trigger happened during subphase 2, the player still carries out subphase 3 but may not choose another worker tile, because no space is left in the job market. If only worker tiles remain, subphase 3 is skipped entirely.
  - The player then carries out subphases 4 and 5 as usual.
  - At the end of the turn, the player refills any empty foresight spaces.
  - This was the active player's last turn.

### 14.2 Final turns

- Each other player receives exactly one final turn. On that turn they move their cattleman as usual and use the reached location.
- A player who reaches Kansas City during their final turn carries out all 5 subphases as usual.
- During subphases 2 and 3 of a final turn, a player may not choose any worker tiles from the foresight spaces. If only worker tiles remain, those subphases are skipped entirely.
- At the end of the final turn, the player still refills any empty foresight spaces.
- After the last player has finished their final turn, the game ends and final scoring begins.

## 15. Final Scoring

Score all 11 categories. The player with the most total victory points wins. In the event of a tie, the victory is shared.

### 15.1 Category 1 — Money

The player gains 1 VP for every full $5 they own. Remainders are ignored.

### 15.2 Category 2 — Private buildings

The player gains the sum of the victory points printed on each of their private building tiles placed on the game board. Unplaced tiles and tiles removed from the game do not score.

### 15.3 Category 3 — City crests

The player gains the sum of the victory points unlocked by their discs on city crests. Negative victory points are subtracted. This includes:

- Victory points printed on city crests that contain the player's discs.
- Victory points printed in green arrows between two adjacent crests, but only if both adjacent crests contain one of the player's discs.
- The **−6 VP** for a disc on the Kansas City crest.

The total can be negative.

### 15.4 Category 4 — Train stations

The player gains the sum of the victory points printed next to each train station that contains one of their discs.

### 15.5 Category 5 — Hazards

The player gains the sum of the victory points printed on each collected hazard tile in front of them (2, 3 or 4 VP per tile).

### 15.6 Category 6 — Cattle cards

The player searches their entire deck — personal draw stack, hand and personal discard pile — for cattle cards with victory-point icons. The player gains the sum of those victory points.

### 15.7 Category 7 — Objective cards

1. For every objective card still in the player's deck, the player decides whether to remove it from the game or add it to the personal objective area now, without using its immediate action.
2. Each objective card in the personal objective area is then checked task by task:
   - If all tasks on the card are fulfilled, the player gains the positive victory points printed on it.
   - If the tasks are not fulfilled completely, the player subtracts the negative victory points printed on it.
3. The result may be negative.

Each single fulfilled task can count towards only one objective card. The starting objective card has no negative value.

### 15.8 Category 8 — Station master tiles

The player gains the sum of the victory points generated by the individual tasks on all station master tiles in front of them. These tasks are independent of objective card tasks.

The possible station master tasks are:

| Task | Victory points |
| :--- | :--- |
| Workers in the worker section | 1 VP per worker, including the workers printed on the player board |
| Objective cards in the personal objective area | 3 VP for every 2 objective cards, whether fulfilled or not |
| Hazard tiles in front of the player | 3 VP for every 2 hazard tiles, of any kind |
| Green + blue teepee pairs | 3 VP for each pair consisting of 1 green and 1 blue teepee tile |
| Certificates | 3 VP for every 2 certificates, permanent or temporary |

### 15.9 Category 9 — Workers in slots 5 and 6

The player gains 4 VP for each worker placed on the fifth or sixth space of any row in the worker section.

### 15.10 Category 10 — Step-limit disc spaces

The player gains 3 VP for each cleared step-limit disc space marked with the 3 VP icon on the player board.

### 15.11 Category 11 — Job market token

The player gains 2 VP if they have the job market token in front of them.

## 16. General Notes and Special Cases

- **Money is unlimited.** If the bank runs out, use a substitute.
- **Objective stack exhaustion:** If the objective stack runs out, vacant spaces in the general objective display are no longer refilled. If the display is empty, no new objective cards can be gained.
- **Discard pile information:** A player may look through their personal discard pile at any time. A player may not look at the cards in their personal draw stack.
- **Deck size:** There is no minimum deck size. A player may remove cards from the game through auxiliary actions to slim the deck. If the deck cannot supply enough cards to reach the hand limit, the player continues with fewer cards.
- **Disc placement fallbacks:** If a disc must be placed on a white-corner space but only dark-corner discs remain, a dark-corner disc may be used. If no disc is available or a required cost cannot be paid, remove one of the player's discs from a train station and place that disc instead.
- **Hand limit and income:** After Kansas City, the entire hand is replenished in Phase C.
- **Cattle market refill:** Only a yellow job-market arrow (or another explicit effect) refills the cattle market. If the market is already at or above its initial size, or the market cattle stack is empty, no cards are added.
- **Hazard and teepee overflow:** If a teepee or hazard tile cannot be placed because its section is full, remove it from the game (return it to the box).
- **Foresight refill:** Each empty foresight space is refilled with a tile whose back number matches the space's number.
- **Building sides:** The side of each private building tile is fixed for the whole game and identical for all players. No player may flip a tile to its other side.
- **Action rewards:** A player may forfeit any reward, completely or partly, except a dollar reward, which must be taken in full.
- **Hand fees:** Only hand fees may be underpaid. All other costs and requirements must be paid or met in full.

## 17. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |

| **Kansas City supply** | The three face-down piles of worker/teepee/hazard tiles sorted by back number 1/2/3. |
| **Foresight space** | One of the 6 Kansas City spaces (2 per spot) holding incoming tiles. |
| **Indian trade section** | The area holding face-up teepee tiles and their money values. |
| **Hazard section** | The flood, drought and rockfall areas, each with spaces numbered 1–4. |
| **General objective display** | The 4 face-up objective cards available to be taken. |
| **Personal objective area** | The face-up objective cards a player has played (or added during final scoring). |
| **White-corner / dark-corner disc** | A disc space with white corners may be placed on any city/station space. A dark-corner disc may only be placed on a dark-corner space, except for the fallback rules. |
| **Active player** | The player whose turn is currently being taken. |
